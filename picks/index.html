Interesting projects I found and picked. None of them are mine: each one credits its authors and links to the original. When I try one myself, I add photos or a video, what I learned and what went wrong.

  • Brushed-metal shine from a filament 3D printer by Chermain et al., SIGGRAPH 2023

    A research method for printing surfaces that shine like brushed metal, whose highlights move as you tilt them, with a standard FDM printer and glossy filament. The direction of the printed lines makes the surface rough in one direction, and that direction controls how light reflects. The paper gives an algorithm that fills a shape with one continuous, closed path whose lines follow the direction you specify at every point.

    How to use it

    1. Prepare the inputs: the shape as an SVG outline, and a grayscale PNG giving the line direction at each point (brightness maps to an angle from −90° to 90°). An optional second PNG picks a mode per area: along the border, across it, smoothest, or as specified.
    2. Run the official code (Python with JAX, CPU or GPU) to generate the path as SVG and G-code. The authors report about 4 minutes for the paper's Figure 1 and about 35 minutes for all results.
    3. Print a single flat layer with glossy (silk or high-gloss) PLA. The included G-code targets a modified Creality CR-10S Pro; the authors used an extrusion factor of 0.9 and recommend a direct-drive extruder.

  • DiscImageStudio by JiaFeiMiao-K-Cat

    Turns an image into disc data. After burning, the picture is visible on the surface of a CD, DVD or single-layer Blu-ray.

    What I want to tryA disc is also a diffraction grating. I want to shine a laser on a burned pattern and see what it projects, then try the reverse: design the pattern from the Fourier transform of the image I want to see.

    How to use it

    1. Download the release for your system from GitHub Releases: an .exe for Windows, an experimental command-line build for Linux and macOS.
    2. Create a project for CD, DVD or Blu-ray, import an image, and adjust dithering, gamma and brightness.
    3. Export an ISO (DVD), WAV + CUE (CD) or a Blu-ray image and burn it with ImgBurn or cdrecord. The author advises using discs you can afford to waste.
    JiaFeiMiao-K-Cat/DiscImageStudio ★ 10.4k · C# · GPL-3.0
